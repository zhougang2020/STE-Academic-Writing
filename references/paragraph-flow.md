# Paragraph Flow Guide

## How to Check Whether Writing Flows

### Strategy 1: Read as an external reader

1. Would a knowledgeable reader understand the vocabulary level?
2. Do body paragraphs connect back to the thesis, and is the connection easy to see?
3. Would someone not in the field understand the main point?

### Strategy 2: Reverse outlining

1. Write down the thesis statement.
2. Write down each topic sentence of each body paragraph.
3. Write down the main evidence or explanation points.
4. Check: topic sentence → thesis, and evidence → topic sentence.
5. Revise or remove any paragraph that cannot be mapped cleanly.

If reverse outlining is easy, the paper is well-organized. If it is hard, the thesis and topic sentences may be unclear.

### Strategy 3: Add temporary headings

Split the draft into sections with headings to see the main parts and how they work together. Remove unnecessary headings before finalizing.

### Strategy 4: Use transition words

Transitions indicate the relationship between thoughts, sentences, or paragraphs:

- Cause and effect: accordingly, as a result, because, consequently, hence, so, then, therefore, thus
- Comparison: also, in the same way, likewise, similarly
- Contrast: although, but, even though, however, in contrast, instead, nevertheless, on the contrary, yet
- Example: for example, for instance, indeed, in fact, such as
- Time or sequence: after, before, eventually, finally, later, meanwhile, next, then
- Summary: as a result, in brief, in conclusion, in short, therefore, thus
